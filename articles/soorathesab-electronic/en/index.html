What Is an Electronic Invoice and Who Must Issue It?What Is an Electronic Invoice and Who Must Issue It?

What is an electronic invoice? This article explains in simple terms what these invoices are, which businesses are required to issue them, and how to get started with the Taxpayers' System (Samaneh-ye Moadian). A complete, practical guide.

24 October 2026

By Dr. Hamed Molatefi, tax adviser · Reviewed 26 October 2026

The electronic invoice is one of the most significant changes to Iran's tax system in recent years. If you run a business and your sales have reached a certain threshold, you are likely required to issue this type of invoice. In this article, we explain in clear, practical language what an electronic invoice is, who is obligated to issue it, and how you can meet this requirement without confusion.

What Is an Electronic Invoice?

An electronic invoice is essentially a structured, digital sales invoice. Unlike traditional paper invoices, these must be issued and transmitted through the Taxpayers' System (Samaneh-ye Moadian) of the Iranian National Tax Administration. The main objective is to increase transaction transparency, prevent tax evasion, and centralize sales data from all businesses.

Every electronic invoice has a unique identifier and is automatically recorded in the system. This identifier enables the tax authority to easily monitor the supply and sales chain.

Electronic Invoices and the Taxpayers' System (Samaneh-ye Moadian)

The Taxpayers' System (Samaneh-ye Moadian) is the platform created by the Iranian National Tax Administration for the registration and exchange of all electronic invoices. If you want to know where to begin, read our article Taxpayers' System (Samaneh-ye Moadian) and Electronic Invoices: Where Do We Start?.

There are three types of electronic invoice. Type 1 carries full seller and buyer details and is used for sales to other businesses (B2B); once the buyer confirms it in their dashboard, it creates a tax credit for the buyer. Type 2 carries full seller and goods/service details, buyer details are optional, it is typically used for sales to final consumers, and it gives the buyer no tax credit. Type 3 is the payment receipt from a card terminal (POS) or online payment gateway, which counts as an electronic invoice under the Tax Administration's rules and gives the buyer no tax credit. Source: Article 1 of the Point-of-Sale Terminals and Taxpayers' System Act, and the Tax Administration's e-invoice data specification.

Who Must Issue Electronic Invoices?

The obligation to issue electronic invoices did not start for everyone at once. The Iranian National Tax Administration has rolled it out in phases, through official "calls" (farakhan) for different groups of taxpayers. Legal entities and sole proprietors become obliged from the date announced in the call or notice that covers their group. To know your own start date, check the Administration's calls and notices and the status of your dashboard (karpusheh).

Smaller businesses may become subject to the requirement in later phases. If you are just starting out or have low turnover, it is best to review your situation with a tax consultant. For small businesses, the Simple Guide to Registering in the Taxpayers' System (Samaneh-ye Moadian) is a good starting point.

Exceptions and Special Considerations

Choosing the Note 100 method for your tax return is not, in itself, an exemption from issuing electronic invoices. When and under what conditions a business must issue e-invoices is set by the Tax Administration's calls and notices; check your status in your dashboard or with an adviser before deciding. The article Note 100 for Business Owners: Who Does It Apply To and What Are the Considerations? provides further details on this topic.

Benefits of Issuing Electronic Invoices

In addition to being a legal obligation, correct issuance of electronic invoices offers many practical advantages, including reduced human error, faster settlement with customers, easier tracking of receivables, and a lower risk of tax penalties.

Businesses that implement this system correctly from the outset will face fewer difficulties when preparing their tax returns. If you make mistakes in your tax declaration, you may receive a tax assessment notice (barg-e tashkhis). The article You Received a Tax Assessment Notice: What to Do First offers useful guidance in this regard.

How to Issue an Electronic Invoice

To issue electronic invoices, you must first register on my.tax.gov.ir and activate your Taxpayers' System (Samaneh-ye Moadian) dashboard. Then obtain a unique tax memory ID and choose how invoices are sent: directly by you, through a trusted tax service provider, or through government systems. After that, you issue and transmit invoices through connected software or directly in your dashboard.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is an electronic invoice and how does it differ from a regular invoice?

An electronic invoice is a structured, digital sales invoice issued and recorded through the Taxpayers' System (Samaneh-ye Moadian) and carries a unique tax identifier.

Which businesses are required to issue electronic invoices?

The obligation has been phased in: legal entities and sole proprietors must issue electronic invoices through the Taxpayers' System (Samaneh-ye Moadian) from the date announced for their group in the Tax Administration's calls or notices.

Do taxpayers under Note 100 also have to issue electronic invoices?

Choosing Note 100 is not, in itself, an exemption from electronic invoicing. Each business's obligation is set by the Tax Administration's calls and notices; check your status in your dashboard or with an adviser.

Where should we start to begin issuing electronic invoices?

First register on my.tax.gov.ir and activate your Taxpayers' System dashboard, then obtain a unique tax memory ID and choose a sending method (direct, trusted provider, or government systems). After that, issue invoices through connected software or directly in your dashboard.

Conclusion

Electronic invoicing is no longer optional; it is part of the requirements of Iran's modern tax system. The sooner you become familiar with it and implement the issuance process in your business, the less likely you are to face penalties and future problems.

Timely action, correct registration in the Taxpayers' System (Samaneh-ye Moadian), and accurate issuance of invoices are the three key factors for success in this area. If you have not yet taken action, take the first step today and seek help from specialists if needed.
