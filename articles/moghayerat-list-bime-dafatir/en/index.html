Insurance list vs books mismatch: what the assessor asksInsurance list vs books mismatch: what the assessor asks

Take an insurance list and books mismatch seriously. See what the assessor compares, and where to start reconciling before you file the return.

17 November 2026

By Dr. Hamed Molatefi, tax adviser · Reviewed 18 November 2026

Short answer

A mismatch in the figures does not, by itself, disallow the entire payroll expense. The tax assessor, however, sets the insurance-list total, the payroll-account movements, and the bank transfers side by side, and treats every undocumented difference as a point of inquiry. If the books exceed the list, the excess without a payslip and a bank transfer record is vulnerable when it comes to accepting the expense. If the list is higher, it must be established whether the cost sits in another account or was never entered in the books at all. The right approach is a monthly reconciliation of headcount and amounts, not a cosmetic adjustment at year-end.

If the figure recorded in the books as salaries and benefits does not match what you have reported each month to the Social Security Organization, that gap will not pass quietly in an audit. A mismatch between the insurance list and the books is one of the issues that surfaces early when personnel costs are reviewed, because the insurance list, the accounting entry, and the bank transfer are three accounts of a single reality. When those accounts do not agree, either part of the expense is called into question or an unnoticed liability shows itself at year-end. Here I take up the question a taxpayer asks at the audit table: how does the tax assessor read this difference, and when should the reconciliation have started?

Why a mismatch between the insurance list and the books is taken seriously in an audit

In a tax audit, payroll expense is not a single figure. The assessor places it beside the lists filed, payroll tax, and the bank movements. If these figures do not agree, the first question is which account is closer to the facts. The difference is not, by itself, a disallowance of the expense, but without a written explanation it will not be read in your favor.

In advisory work we have often seen the list filed and the entry recorded, yet no one has reconciled the annual totals. Each month looked roughly right on its own, but the New Year bonus (Eidi), overtime, a list correction, or a transfer of cost to a project account opened a gap in the annual total. The assessor sees that gap, not your intention.

The practical question is not whether you filed the list. It is whether, if the monthly totals for the year are set against the payroll-account movements, you have a defensible sentence for every difference. That sentence must lead to evidence: the internal payroll list, the contract, a description of the benefits, and the bank transfer record. An oral explanation in the meeting does not take the place of that file.

The insurance list and the books are not the same document

In an audit the two are placed side by side, but they are not the same thing. If that distinction is not clear, you either treat the expense as identical to the insurance list, or you assume that filing the list means the books have been fully recorded.

What the insurance list shows

The insurance list is your report to the Social Security Organization: who is covered, and what wage has been stated as the base for the contribution. Filing is done through the Social Security e-services portal and does not replace the accounting entry. Take which payments are subject to contribution withholding from the Social Security Organization and the current circulars published there, not from a list that has circulated in a working group for years.

What obligation the books and the return create

The books must show the financial reality: salaries and benefits expense, the employer's share, amounts payable to employees, and amounts payable to the Organization. The form of that entry is set by accounting standards. For the recording framework, refer to the Audit Organization, not to a ready-made sample entry.

The return is the annual account of those same books. If the books state one figure and the return another, the return is fragile even before any comparison with insurance. Ask about the criteria for an acceptable expense on the Iranian National Tax Administration information portal, and in light of your own file. I am not setting out a definitive list of what is accepted and what is rejected here; the detail depends on the current circular and the type of activity.

Three common forms of discrepancy

A difference does not always mean the same thing. Before any correction, identify the nature of the discrepancy. The three situations below are the ones seen most often in an audit.

When the books exceed the list

That means there is a cost in the accounts that is not in the insurance report. Sometimes there is an explainable reason: a payment that did not appear on that month's list, or a benefit that needs its own description. If you have no payslip and no transfer for this excess, you are exposed on acceptance of the expense, and that same payment may later be treated as unreported wages. I am not ruling on coverage here. The authority is the portal and, where needed, the branch.

When the list exceeds the books

Do not treat this case lightly. Sometimes the cost has been posted to overhead or a project instead of the payroll account, and the books are right overall; only the classification is scattered. Sometimes the payment was made and never entered — for example, from a personal account. In the second case the financial picture is incomplete, and the problem is not only insurance. If business money has left a household account, start the reconciliation by separating that path. In the article Difference between a personal account and a business account we explain why mixing the two tangles both insurance and tax.

When the totals are close but a month or a person has shifted

The assessor does not look only at the year-end total, and samples individual months. One person who is on the insurance list but not on the payroll list, or a cost posted in the wrong month, creates as much trouble as a difference in the totals. Do not treat contract labour as the same as a worker covered by the list. We have drawn the line between these withholdings in Withholdings on a contracting agreement, so that salary, retention, and contract insurance do not fall into one column.

Reconcile every month

Reconciliation is not a job for the night before the return. Each month, after the payroll list is prepared and before the entry is closed, four checks are enough. Set that month's list total from the e-services portal beside the gross total of the internal list. Match people name by name, not only the rial total. Write down every difference the same week: the nature of the payment, why it is missing from the list or from the entry, and the supporting document. Agree the net payable with the bank transfer.

On the monthly worksheet these columns are enough: name, gross total of the internal list, wage total of the insurance list, net bank transfer, and a description of the difference. Tie the column totals to that month's entry and keep the worksheet with the books. In an audit this sheet usually moves the matter forward faster than an oral explanation. Keep the employer's share separate from wages, so it is later clear which part is salary and which part is the insurance contribution.

The annual control point is before the return is filed. For business owners this deadline is the end of Khordad of the following year, and for a legal entity whose financial year ends in Esfand it is the end of Tir. The Iranian National Tax Administration has allowed 15 days after the end of the quarter for VAT, and 45 days after the end of the quarter for quarterly transaction reports (Article 169); do not confuse these with the insurance-list due date. Check the latest position in the Tax compliance calendar and in the Administration's notices. Take the filing day of the list itself from the e-services portal, and do not write a fixed date from memory.

A practical example: a hypothetical workshop and the year-end gap

This scenario is hypothetical and contains no real name. The owner of a small manufacturing workshop, while closing the year, found that total payroll expense in the books exceeded the total wages on the insurance lists. The lists had been filed regularly and the payroll entry recorded every month, but no one had set the two side by side during the year.

The first step was to break the discrepancy down by month, rather than argue over a single annual total. Most of the gap sat in months when Eidi and several one-off payments had been recorded in the books, with no trace of them on that month's insurance list.

The second step was to separate documented payments from unclear ones. For part of the amount there was a description of the benefits and a bank-transfer receipt. For another part there was only a line on the expense entry: no clear contract, no payslip, and no bank transfer record that could be tied to the same person.

The third step is to picture the audit meeting. The assessor first asks for the annual total, then opens the months with the largest differences. The documented portion can be explained, although the insurance treatment of that payment must be asked of the Social Security Organization, not guessed in the meeting. The undocumented portion is where acceptance of the expense is in dispute, and the line "we will fix it later" is not enough there.

The fourth step is the work that should have been done that same month. If a reconciliation worksheet had existed, that unclear payment would either have been documented, or would not have been posted to payroll expense, or, if it really was wages, its reporting path would also have been clear. The knot in this hypothetical example was several weeks of delay across the year, not a sudden event in Esfand.

What not to do if you spot the discrepancy late

When a discrepancy is spotted late, the temptation is to post an unsupported entry so the figures match, or to build a list that does not agree with the payments actually made. That does not erase the discrepancy, and it also makes the books' account indefensible. Correcting the insurance list has its own procedure on the Social Security portal. Take the deadline and the documents from that same portal.

On the tax side your hands are not free either. An amended return is possible only until the end of the legal deadline for filing that same return. After that, do not count on a free correction. If the discrepancy runs into a liability and a penalty, that path is separate from reconciling the books. We have set out how the penalty arises and how a waiver is requested in the article Tax late-payment penalty, and we are not mixing it here with correcting the list.

If you are not sure whether the difference comes from account classification or from an undocumented expense, sit down with the file's adviser before any correction. Guessing at this point usually costs more than a short review.

Frequently Asked Questions

If payroll in the books exceeds the insurance list, is the expense disallowed?

Not necessarily. An excess backed by a payslip, a contract, and a bank transfer can be explained, but an undocumented figure is vulnerable in an audit. Ask the Social Security Organization whether that payment is covered for insurance; do not close both sides with a single guess.

I have filed the list every month. Do I still need to reconcile it with the books?

Yes. Filing the list means reporting to Social Security, not that the accounting entry has automatically matched it. A name-by-name and rial-total reconciliation is a separate task, and it should be done the same month.

Can I change the book entry so the figures match?

Only if the correction reflects the payment that was actually made. An unsupported entry or a fabricated list does not erase the discrepancy, and it makes the books worse in an audit. Pursue any list correction through the Social Security portal.

How long do I have to bring this reconciliation into the return?

Preferably every month. If it has reached the return, a correction is possible only until the end of the legal deadline for filing that same return. For business owners this deadline is the end of Khordad of the following year, and for a legal entity whose year ends in Esfand it is the end of Tir. After the deadline, do not count on a free correction.

Summary

A mismatch between the insurance list and the books is not, by itself, a finding against you, but without an explanation and supporting documents it will be read against you in an audit. The list, the entry, and the bank must be able to tell one story. Do not leave this until Esfand. Each month, reconcile headcount and amounts, and put the difference in writing the same week.

Take insurance coverage from the Social Security Organization, the criteria for an acceptable expense from the Iranian National Tax Administration, and the form of the entry from accounting standards. If the discrepancy is on your desk now, do not put off the review. To walk through this reconciliation, you can book time through tax advisory services, so the file explaining the differences is ready before the audit.
