Note 100 for Business Owners: Who Qualifies and What Considerations Apply?Note 100 for Business Owners: Who Qualifies and What Considerations Apply?

Note 100 offers a simpler route, but it is not suitable for everyone. First assess the revenue ceiling, type of activity, and your tax record.

25 April 2026

By Dr. Hamed Molatefi, tax adviser · Reviewed 27 October 2026

Every year, as the tax return deadline approaches, the same phrase is heard frequently: “Note 100 is simpler; let’s go with that.” Simplicity is appealing, yet choosing simplicity without proper assessment can sometimes make next year’s file more vulnerable. Note 100 is an option, not a universal rule.

First, it must be understood that this route is primarily intended for certain business owners with specific income conditions. Legal entities are generally not the target audience. Even among sole proprietors, the type of activity, method of sales, and the revenue ceiling announced for that year are important. The income ceiling and implementation details are announced each year in the Tax Administration's instructions or notices, and last year's figure may differ this year. The deadline is the same as the self-employed income tax return: under Article 100 of the Direct Taxes Act, by the end of Khordad of the following year, with registration and payment through my.tax.gov.ir.

The second consideration is the quality of your tax history. If irregular sales were recorded in previous years, or there are discrepancies between bank transactions and declared amounts, choosing the simplified route will not necessarily eliminate those gaps. The Tax Authority may later examine other indicators. Selecting Note 100 does not end all inquiries; it merely changes the form of compliance for that year.

The third consideration is how this choice aligns with the Taxpayers' System (Samaneh-ye Moadian) and invoices. If a significant portion of sales has been recorded in the system, the figure you have in mind for Note 100 must be consistent with that data. Ignoring this alignment can make future explanations difficult.

The fourth consideration concerns the actual expenses of the business. Some taxpayers incur substantial costs and wish to reflect them in their return. In simplified routes, there is less room to display detailed expenses. If showing costs and depreciation is analytically important to you, the full route may provide greater clarity, even if it takes a little more time.

The fifth consideration is peace of mind. Some business owners simply want to close the matter for the current year and return to their core activities. If the legal conditions are met and the declared amount is not unreasonably distant from actual sales, Note 100 can deliver exactly that peace of mind. The key is that the decision must be informed, not made in a rush during the final week.

In practice, before making any recommendation we place three documents side by side: the sales picture for the year, the main cost picture, and the picture of parallel obligations such as value-added tax or seasonal transactions. When these three speak to each other, choosing between Note 100 and a full return is no longer guesswork.

Tax consulting with a long-term perspective means closing this year’s file in a way that does not force you into hasty explanations next year. Note 100 is a tool. A tool is only useful when it fits your hand.

Frequently Asked Questions

Who is the 100th note suitable for?

The 100th note is mainly meaningful for some business owners with specific income conditions. Legal entities are usually not the audience for this space. The type of activity, method of sale, and the ceiling announced in the same year are important.

Does choosing the 100th note solve all tax problems?

Choosing the note is not the end of the question; it only changes the form of the obligation for that year. If irregular sales were recorded in previous years or there is a gap between bank turnover and declaration, choosing the simple path does not necessarily clear that gap. The organization may later look at other evidence.

How do we decide whether to choose the 100th note or file a full tax return?

Before any recommendation, put three sheets together: the image of the year's sales, the image of the main costs, and the image of parallel obligations such as value added tax or seasonal transactions. If real costs and depreciation are analytically important to you, the more complete path may be clearer. The decision must be made knowingly, not in a rush in the last week.
