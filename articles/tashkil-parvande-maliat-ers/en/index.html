Opening an Inheritance Tax File: From Death to SettlementOpening an Inheritance Tax File: From Death to Settlement

Read how to open an inheritance tax file, from documents to settlement. See the administrative path, required papers, and when to consult an adviser.

6 November 2026

By Dr. Hamed Molatefi, tax adviser · Reviewed 10 November 2026

Short answer

Opening an inheritance tax file starts with the death certificate, a document establishing the heirs, and an inventory of assets. Then check the official portal to see whether electronic filing is open for you or you must go to the competent office. Until the tax is assessed and paid, transfer of many assets—such as an account, a vehicle, or a title deed—is usually not completed. Take the rate, exemptions, and deadline from the official system or an adviser. This text is the path of the work, not a definitive figure.

If someone in your family has died this week, the first sentence you are likely to hear from the bank or the notary office is that assets will not move until the tax position is clear. Opening an inheritance tax file is exactly that starting point: identifying the heirs, listing what the deceased left, and placing the matter on the official path of the Iranian National Tax Administration. At Forough Shomar Simorgh we have often seen a family held up for months over one missing document or a deadline heard from other people. This text shows how the work proceeds; the rate, exemptions, and binding dates must come from an official source.

Where exactly does opening an inheritance tax file begin?

Inheritance tax attaches to the estate (matrakeh)—what remains after death—not to the heirs' later income. Opening a file means the Administration knows who the deceased was, who the heirs are, what each person's share is, and which assets and liabilities can be examined. Until that picture is on paper, any figure the family calculates cannot be relied on.

Do not start from memory or from an old file. The official point of entry is the National Electronic Tax Services Portal (my.tax.gov.ir). Sign-in is with a national ID number, a password, and an SMS code—the same path used for other tax services, not a separate system under another name. If, on an inheritance matter, the portal directs you to an in-person visit or to a specific office, do not treat that as an error. Which office has jurisdiction, and the form of registration, follow the Administration's current practice.

Either each heir acts separately from their own city, or one person registers everything without coordinating with the others. Either course can lock the file. Before you file, decide who will follow the matter, and if that person signs for the others, they should hold a formal power of attorney. Take the limits of that authority from the administering office, or from the text of the law on the National Laws and Regulations System (official text of the laws), not from the family group.

Complete the documents before you attend

The office does not build a file on an oral account. For anything you cannot show, write it so it is clear you do not yet have the document—not that the asset or the liability does not exist. That candor at the start is cheaper than a late correction.

Death and heirship documents

Without a death certificate and a document that shows the heirs and each person's share, the work effectively does not move. In visits we have attended with the taxpayer, the certificate of heirship (govahi-ye enhesar-e verasat) is the first paper the officer asks for. If that certificate has not yet been issued, do not start from a list of names on paper. First settle heirship before the judicial authority, then come to the tax office. Keep the identity booklet (shenasnameh) and national ID card of the deceased and of the heirs together, even if you have only duplicates.

Inventory of assets and liabilities

Categorize the asset list, and do not write a final price on it yourself. Set out separately real property, vehicles, deposits and accounts, shares and partnership interests, a shop or an active business, amounts owed by others, safe-deposit boxes, and valuables. For property, the deed particulars and the address are enough. For an account, keep a bank letter or the balance around the date of death. For a vehicle, the ownership document and the plate number. If the deceased had a business activity, keep the activity records and any provable liabilities separate, and do not assume those obligations closed at death. The logic of preparing documents for an examination is the same; see the detail in Documents required for a tax examination, before the assessor arrives.

Do not state a liability only orally. A debt that exists only in a sibling's memory usually carries no weight. Support each liability with a contract, a receipt, or a bank statement, and do not expect an automatic deduction. Whether a liability is accepted or rejected is decided in the examination. If the evidence is incomplete, write that down, so you are not later accused of concealment.

Filing the case and following it on the official path

Once the documents are gathered, return to the portal and see whether the inheritance service is open for you. If electronic filing is active, upload clear files and save the receipt at once. Name each file so that two months later you still know which document it is. If the system refers you to attend in person, go with the original documents and note the case file number or tracking code before you leave the office. Do not rely on a staff member's memory or on an oral assurance.

Treat the deadline as serious from the date of death, but do not take the figure from this article. The statute and circulars may set a time window for filing, for payment, or for certain reliefs, and that window can change with a new circular. See current due dates in the Tax Obligations Calendar and read the latest news on the information site of the Iranian National Tax Administration. If the deceased had an active business, an unfiled return, or a finalized liability, the inheritance file does not replace those obligations. Follow the two in separate folders.

A worked example: a hypothetical file from death to the asset list

The example is hypothetical, and the names are not real. Suppose Mr. K has died in a central city and has three heirs: a spouse and two children. The known assets are these: one residential apartment, one passenger car, and one bank account. He had no active business, and the family documents show no bank debt.

Step one is for the heirs to obtain the death certificate and apply for a certificate of heirship. Until each person's share is on official paper, none of them should tell the bank that they will settle everything themselves.

Step two: one of the children, in coordination with the other two, writes the list in three lines—the apartment deed particulars, the vehicle particulars, and the bank's name together with the balance from the latest text message or passbook. They do not enter the apartment's current market price. Beside the property they note that the official authority will determine the value, so they are not later caught up in a figure of their own making.

Step three: they check the electronic service on the portal. If remote filing is open, they send a legible scan and save the receipt in a shared folder. If an in-person referral comes through, they go the same day with the original documents and note the tracking code.

Step four: they do not sign a preliminary sale agreement (gholnameh) for the apartment until the tax position is determined. Inheritance tax is not the same as the tax that arises on a later transfer of the property. Read that difference in Property transfer tax and the documents it requires so you do not count payment of one as payment of the other.

Do not treat settlement as the same thing as transferring the asset

Settlement means the tax determined after examination is paid, and you obtain a document that removes the bar on transfer. The title of that document may not be the same for the bank, the traffic police, and the notary office. Ask each authority exactly which paper it wants.

I have deliberately not written the rates and exemptions. The type of asset and the heir's relationship to the deceased matter in the law and may be updated. Take the calculation from the office or from tax advisory services, not from figures passed from hand to hand.

If you do not accept the tax figure

If a notice arrives that takes a different view of the property's value, or of whether an asset is within scope, do not set it aside. For a tax assessment notice (barg-e tashkhis), under Article 238 of the Direct Taxes Act, you have 30 days from the date of service to attend or to object, and after that it becomes final. Read how that window works in Objecting to a tax assessment notice (barg-e tashkhis) and the decisive 30 days. What governs is the date of lawful service, not the day one of the heirs sent the notice in the family group. One person should be responsible for watching for service.

Mistakes that push settlement back

Hiding an account or a vehicle in the hope that no one knows damages the credibility of the file; registry and bank records are usually seen sooner or later. Transferring an asset before settlement, and abandoning the file after filing, are two costly mistakes. Opening the file is not the end of the work. Take the latest rule from the Iranian National Tax Administration, not from a dated article.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can you start an inheritance tax file without a certificate of heirship?

In practice, no. The office must identify the heirs and each person's share from an official document. Obtain the certificate of heirship first, then take the tax file forward.

Must every heir open a separate file?

Not necessarily. Usually one person following up, in coordination with the others and, if needed, with a formal power of attorney, is enough. Before you file, ask the office or the official portal how signatures should be made.

If we do not agree with the inheritance tax amount, how long do we have?

For a tax assessment notice (barg-e tashkhis), under Article 238 of the Direct Taxes Act, you have 30 days from the date of service to attend or to object. After that period the notice becomes final. The date of a family message does not govern.

Does paying inheritance tax also reduce the tax on a later sale of the property to zero?

No. These are two separate obligations. Settling the inheritance does not remove the tax that arises on a later transfer of the property.

Summary

Opening an inheritance tax file stays short when heirship is clear, the asset list is complete, and the rate and the deadline come from an official source. Rushing a transfer usually costs more than one extra visit. If the estate spans several cities, an active business is involved, or the heirs disagree over the shares, take advice at the outset.
