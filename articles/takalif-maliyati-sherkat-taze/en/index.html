New Company Tax Duties: From Registration to First ReturnNew Company Tax Duties: From Registration to First Return

Read the tax duties of a newly formed company from registration day to the tax return and invoices, and start year one without doubt or confusion.

20 November 2026

By Dr. Hamed Molatefi, tax adviser · Reviewed 20 November 2026

Short answer

Registering a company does not, by itself, create a tax file. First complete the Iranian National Tax Administration portal, open your Taxpayers' System (Samaneh-ye Moadian) workspace, and obtain a unique tax memory ID. At the same time, separate the company account from personal accounts and write up the books from the first financial event. Issue electronic invoices only if a rollout call covers you, and do not issue an invoice in the main workspace for testing.

The tax duties of a newly formed company start on the day you received the national ID and the formation notice was published, not on the day of the first customer receipt. We have often seen a company finish registration and assume there is time until year-end, then enter an audit with mixed accounts and a late return. We do not invent a rate or a circular we are not sure is correct, and we refer the details to the official system.

From which day are a newly formed company's tax duties counted?

Many taxpayers leave a gap between registering the company and opening the tax file. Year one has three layers: registration and the notice; entry to the portal and the workspace; and reporting, which covers the books and, where applicable, invoices and the return. If the second layer falls behind, the third closes on incomplete evidence.

Follow the formation notice in the Official Gazette of the Islamic Republic of Iran and keep a legible copy with the national ID and the articles of association. In a first-year audit, the assessor often wants these same documents to see from what date activity began and what the company's object is. Align the objects clause with the real activity. If you sell services and the articles cover only trading, you will later run into trouble with the activity code and invoices.

One point we always raise in the first meeting: do not leave the financial year to chance. For companies whose financial year ends in Esfand, the income tax return deadline is 4 months after the end of the financial year and runs through the end of Tir. If you have a different financial year, count those same 4 months from the end of your own financial year. Also see the deadlines in the tax duties calendar; this page is not a substitute for a circular.

Opening the file on the Iranian National Tax Administration portal

The registration path starts at the my.tax.gov.ir portal, not the government single window. Sign-in uses a national ID code, a password, and an SMS code. After registration, open the Taxpayers' System (Samaneh-ye Moadian) workspace at tp.tax.gov.ir, obtain the unique tax memory ID, and choose a submission method: direct, through a trusted service provider, or via government systems. Weigh these three methods against invoice volume and the office team's capacity, not against a software vendor's pitch.

Read up-to-date rollout calls and notices on the Iranian National Tax Administration information portal. What is passed around in a messaging group may relate to an earlier rollout phase. Until you have seen the notice on an official source, do not treat the duty as settled for your own company.

There is no test invoice in the main workspace, and every invoice issued is real. Do not issue an invoice on the main workspace to test software. Ask the software firm for a test environment, and start live submission only when the buyer, the amount, and the service description are correct.

Choosing the submission method

If invoices are few, direct submission may be enough. With several users or daily sales, a trusted service provider or a software connection makes more sense. We have set out the criteria in the article on choosing accounting software for the Taxpayers' System (Samaneh-ye Moadian). The software must build the invoice correctly, not merely offer a send button.

Separate the company's money from day one

The most common breakdown in year one is mixing the founder's personal account with the company account. If initial capital, petty cash, the director's drawings, and customer sales all land on one card, you must explain each transaction in an audit. We have set out this distinction in the article on the difference between a personal account and a business account. From week 1, open an account in the company's name, and let company sales and expenses pass only through that account.

Write up the books from the first financial event, even if it is only a capital deposit. An expense with no invoice or bank receipt does not readily remain allowable in an audit. Check the reporting framework with the Audit Organization, and if a certified public accountant's signature is required, get to know the Iranian Association of Certified Public Accountants. We have covered the year-end close in financial statements for the income tax return; a newly formed company has no prior-year record in which to hide an error.

Do not record a founder's drawing as a company expense. If the director takes money from the company account for personal spending, that is a drawing and must appear in the partners' current account. This mistake is common in year one, because the founder still sees the company as a second pocket.

Judge electronic invoices against the rollout call

The duty to issue electronic invoices has been implemented in stages, through rollout calls by the Iranian National Tax Administration. Judge whether you are covered from the rollout call, not from a guess or a channel message. First see which stage your company falls in, then start submission.

If you become covered, match the invoice type to the buyer type. A Type 1 invoice carries full seller and buyer details, is for sales to economic operators, and creates a VAT credit for the buyer. Type 2 is usually a sale to a final consumer; entering the buyer's details is optional, and it carries no VAT credit. Type 3 is a card-reader or payment-gateway receipt, and it likewise carries no VAT credit. Do not replace a sale to another company with a card-reader receipt; the buyer later receives no credit, and the commercial relationship suffers.

Note 100 is a path for some business owners, not a shortcut for a company, and it is not an exemption from electronic invoices. A legal entity has its own duties. If the founder separately carries on a sole-proprietor business, do not mix that file with the company's file.

Deadlines forgotten in year one

Legal entities have up to 4 months after the end of the financial year to file the income tax return. If the financial year ends in Esfand, that deadline runs through the end of Tir. An amended return can be submitted only until the end of that same legal deadline. After the deadline has passed, you cannot tidy an error with an amendment. Close the draft early so that, if you spot a mismatch, you are still inside the deadline.

If you are covered by VAT, each season's return is due 15 days after the end of the season. The Article 169 seasonal transactions report, if you are covered, is due up to 45 days after the end of the season. Do not mix the two. The sales figure must agree with the books and the invoices submitted. If you are not sure you are covered, check the workspace and the official notice before setting the duty aside.

We do not give a penalty figure here; read the rate and any relief from the official source for that period. A delay, even if it can be followed up later, takes the director's time and the company's money. Put the deadline on the calendar, not in memory.

A practical example: the first 3 months of a hypothetical company

This scenario is entirely hypothetical and does not refer to any real person or company. Suppose a hypothetical services company was registered at the beginning of Mehr, its object is software, and its financial year ends at the end of Esfand.

In week 1 it archives the national ID and the notice, registers on the portal, and obtains the unique tax memory ID. It does not yet issue invoices until rollout-call coverage is clear. It opens the company account and pays the capital only into that account.

In week 3 it signs a contract with another company. If it is covered and the buyer is an economic operator, it issues a Type 1 invoice. If it is not yet covered, it records the sale only in the books and does not issue a test invoice.

At the end of the season it reconciles sales, the bank, and the books. If covered, it files VAT within the 15-day deadline and the seasonal transactions within the 45-day deadline, separately, and closes the income tax return before the end of Tir. Because registration was mid-year, the first period is shorter than 12 months.

Frequently Asked Questions

I have registered the company. Must I issue electronic invoices from that same day?

Not necessarily. The electronic-invoice duty has been implemented in stages by rollout call, and it is not settled for every company from the day of registration. Check your status in the workspace and in Iranian National Tax Administration notices, then start submission.

Can I issue a test invoice in the workspace to test the software?

No. There is no test invoice in the main workspace, and every invoice issued is real. Run the test outside the main workspace.

When is the income tax return deadline for a newly formed company?

Legal entities have up to 4 months after the end of the financial year. If the financial year ends in Esfand, the deadline runs through the end of Tir. An amended return is also possible only until the end of that same deadline.

Does Note 100 exempt the company from electronic invoices?

No. Note 100 is not an exemption from electronic invoices, and the path for business owners should not be equated with a legal entity's duty.

Summary

A newly formed company makes year one calm or costly with three decisions: opening the file on time, separating the company's money from the founder's money, and reporting only to the extent of the actual duty.
